# First-Offer Email Template — Show Your Math

Why this works: most low offers get deleted because they look like a
lowball with nothing behind it. This email shows the seller (or their
agent) the math behind your number — their own fixed-up value and a
real repair figure — so the offer reads as arithmetic, not an insult.
Write it fresh for every property. Never send the same wording twice;
sellers and agents compare notes.

## The template

> Subject: Written offer — [property address]
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I'm a local buyer looking at [address]. I buy as-is, cash, and I can
> work on your timeline — no repairs, no cleanout, no showings to
> schedule around.
>
> So the number makes sense instead of just looking low, here's my math:
> fixed up, I put the value around $[their fixed-up value]. The work it
> needs — [name the two or three biggest items] — runs about
> $[repair figure]. After that work and the costs of carrying and
> reselling it, I can pay **$[your offer]** and close in [timeframe].
>
> I work with a cash buyer on the funding side, and I'm not represented
> by a buyer's agent. The paperwork is a standard purchase agreement,
> and I'm glad to walk through any line of it.
>
> If the number is off what you had in mind, tell me where it needs to
> be — I'd rather hear a counter than guess. Either way, thanks for
> reading this far.
>
> [Your name]
> [Phone]

## Rules that come with it

1. **The math must be real.** Use your actual MAO numbers (see the MAO
   cheat sheet in this kit). A seller who checks your comps and finds
   them honest takes the next call.
2. **Name real defects.** "The roof and the kitchen" beats "repairs."
   It proves you actually looked.
3. **One next step.** A counter, a call, a yes — end with exactly one.
4. **Follow up once, with something new.** Never resend the same email.
   A follow-up adds a fact — a comp, a timeline offer, a term — it
   never just says "checking in."
5. **Know your state's rules before you send.** Wholesaling disclosure
   and assignment rules differ by state, and a few states restrict the
   practice. Verify yours first. (The full store library includes
   state-by-state compliance notes.)
