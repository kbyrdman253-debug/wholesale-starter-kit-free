# MAO Cheat Sheet — The Maximum Allowable Offer

Your MAO is the highest price you can put under contract and still leave
room for your assignment fee and your buyer's profit. Offer above it and
the deal dies later. Offer at or under it and the deal can actually close.

## The formula

```
MAO = (ARV × Discount) − Repairs − Assignment Fee
```

- **ARV** — After Repair Value: what the property will be worth fully
  fixed up. Build it from the top 3 comparable sales near the property
  (within 0.5 miles urban, 2 miles rural), sold in the last 90 days,
  similar square footage (±20%). Average them. Drop outliers.
- **Discount** — 0.70 is the standard working number. It is what leaves
  your buyer their margin. Don't quietly raise it to make a deal "work."
- **Repairs** — your honest repair estimate. Specific defects, not a
  round number you liked.
- **Assignment Fee** — your fee. $100 is the floor, not the target —
  take as much as the spread honestly supports, but never kill a
  penciling deal over fee size.

## Worked example

A house that will be worth **$200,000** fixed up. It needs **$35,000** in
repairs. You want a **$10,000** assignment fee.

```
MAO = (200,000 × 0.70) − 35,000 − 10,000
    = 140,000 − 35,000 − 10,000
    = $95,000
```

$95,000 is your ceiling. Offer $95,000 or walk under it — never above.

Quick screen when you don't have comps yet: roughly **60% of the asking
price** on a stale, as-is listing tells you whether it's worth running
full comps at all. It's a screen, not an offer.

## Land is different

Raw land has no ARV in the house sense. Working rule: price off a
fraction of the area's new-build median value, minus a site allowance
(utilities, clearing, access), minus your fee. Land math is thinner and
less forgiving — verify utilities and water before you trust any number.

## Flag it, don't guess it

If any of these are unknown, your MAO is **not finished** — write
"MISSING" next to it and go get it:

- Repair scope (what exactly is broken?)
- Seller's payoff / mortgage balance
- HOA dues, liens, back taxes
- Closing costs
- Unpermitted additions or unverified utilities

A flagged gap protects you. A guessed number becomes your problem at
closing.
