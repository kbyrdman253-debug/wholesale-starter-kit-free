# The 4-Pillars Seller Call Checklist

A lead is not qualified until you have confirmed answers in all four
pillars. Miss one and you don't have a deal — you have a phone call.
Print this. Keep it next to the phone. Fill every line before you make
an offer.

## Pillar 1 — Condition

Specific defects, not "it needs work."

- [ ] Roof: age, leaks, layers?
- [ ] Foundation / structure: cracks, settling, water in basement?
- [ ] Kitchen & baths: usable as-is, or gut jobs?
- [ ] HVAC, plumbing, electrical: working? Age?
- [ ] Cosmetic: paint, floors, fixtures — what's the real scope?
- [ ] Anything unpermitted? Fire, flood, or code issues?

## Pillar 2 — Timeline

An exact date beats "soon" every time.

- [ ] When do they want to be done and gone by? (Get a date.)
- [ ] What happens if that date passes? (That's the real deadline.)
- [ ] Already listed / tried to sell? How long, what happened?

## Pillar 3 — Motivation

The surface reason is rarely the real one. Ask, listen, ask again —
the actual driver usually shows up two or three layers down.

- [ ] Why are they selling at all?
- [ ] What's making it urgent — money, health, a move, a tenant, a death
      in the family, a divorce?
- [ ] What does "solved" look like for them — speed, certainty, not
      having to clean/repair, not dealing with showings?

## Pillar 4 — Price

- [ ] What number did they say first? (Their anchor — write it down.)
- [ ] Walk them down gently: "If we could take the repairs and the
      timeline off your plate, what's the lowest you'd walk away with?"
- [ ] The lowest number they'll actually accept: ____________
- [ ] Mortgage/payoff still owed? ____________  HOA/liens/taxes? ____

## Before you hang up — the lock

End every call with one concrete next step and a time on it:

> "I'll run the numbers tonight and call you tomorrow at [time] with a
> written offer. If the math doesn't work, I'll tell you that too."

Then do it. Silence is where deals go to die — a seller who doesn't hear
back assumes you were never serious.
